# Computational analysis of human adipose snMultiome data

## snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq preprocessing and quality control

Single-nucleus multiome data were processed on a per-sample basis using Seurat and Signac in R. Gene-expression matrices, ATAC peak matrices and fragment files were obtained from the 10x Genomics output for each sample. For RNA quality control, genes detected in fewer than two nuclei were removed at object creation. Nuclei were retained when they contained at least 400 RNA counts, at least 100 and no more than 6,000 detected RNA features, and no more than 15% mitochondrial transcripts. Putative doublets were identified using scDblFinder and excluded.

ATAC quality-control metrics were calculated with Signac. Chromatin assays were generated using the hg38 reference genome, and gene annotations were obtained from EnsDb.Hsapiens.v86. Nucleosome signal and transcription start site (TSS) enrichment were calculated using NucleosomeSignal and TSSEnrichment. For the combined RNA-ATAC quality-control step, nuclei were required to have at least 100 ATAC counts, at least 50 detected ATAC features, a TSS enrichment score of at least 2, and a nucleosome signal of no more than 4, in addition to the RNA criteria above.

Ambient RNA contamination was corrected after final quality control using SoupX. For each sample, retained nuclei were normalized and briefly reclustered to provide cluster information for SoupX. Contamination fractions were estimated with autoEstCont and corrected count matrices were generated using adjustCounts. Corrected per-sample objects were subsequently merged. Sample CS4 was excluded from the final manuscript analysis based on the initial quality assessment; the final dataset comprised 31,808 nuclei from seven individuals (four Cushing syndrome and three control individuals).

## Construction of the integrated multiome object

A common ATAC peak set was generated by combining sample-specific peak calls, merging overlapping intervals, and retaining peaks between 20 bp and 10 kb in width. Peaks on standard chromosomes (chr1-22, chrX and chrY) were retained. Fragment counts for the union peak set were quantified for each sample using FeatureMatrix and combined into a common chromatin assay.

RNA data were normalized with NormalizeData, variable features were identified with FindVariableFeatures, scaled with ScaleData and reduced by principal-component analysis using 30 principal components. ATAC data were transformed using TF-IDF, all peaks were retained as features using FindTopFeatures with a q0 cutoff, and latent semantic indexing was performed using singular-value decomposition. Patient-associated variation was reduced independently in the RNA and ATAC modalities using Harmony with the sample identifier (orig.ident) as the grouping variable. Weighted nearest-neighbor (WNN) analysis was then performed using RNA Harmony dimensions 1-30 and ATAC Harmony dimensions 2-30. The integrated WNN graph was used for UMAP visualization and graph-based clustering. Broad cell populations were assigned manually based on established marker expression and included adipocytes, adipose stromal/progenitor cells (ASPCs), endothelial cells, smooth muscle cells, pericytes, myeloid cells, lymphoid cells, lymphatic endothelial cells and Schwann cells. Nuclei assigned to adrenal tissue were excluded from the main adipose-tissue analysis.

## ASPC subclustering and population abundance

ASPCs were extracted from the integrated multiome object and RNA and ATAC modalities were reprocessed using the same normalization, dimensional-reduction, Harmony and WNN workflow. WNN clustering was performed at a resolution of 0.4. The resulting 4,739 ASPCs were classified into three populations based on their transcriptional and multiomic profiles: DPP4+ ASPCs, characterized by DPP4 and PI16; CXCL14+ ASPCs, characterized by CXCL14 and COL15A1; and PPARG+ ASPCs, characterized by PPARG and FABP4.

Population abundances were calculated separately for each patient to retain the patient as the biological replicate. Differences between Cushing syndrome and control groups were assessed using two-sided Welch's t-tests. In addition to individual population frequencies, the CXCL14+/PPARG+ balance was summarized as the log2 ratio of the corresponding patient-level proportions.

## Adipogenic trajectory and pseudotime analysis

To model adipogenic progression, the ASPC populations were combined with mature adipocytes. To prevent the substantially larger adipocyte compartment from dominating the trajectory, mature adipocytes were downsampled to a maximum of 300 nuclei per patient, resulting in 2,100 mature adipocytes and 6,839 nuclei in the final trajectory object. A constrained adipogenic trajectory was fitted with Slingshot using the WNN UMAP representation and the ordered lineage DPP4+ ASPCs to CXCL14+ ASPCs to PPARG+ ASPCs to mature adipocytes. DPP4+ ASPCs were used as the starting population and mature adipocytes as the terminal population. Slingshot pseudotime values were retained for downstream analyses.

For visualization within the ASPC compartment, saved pseudotime values were transferred by cell barcode to the ASPC WNN object and displayed on the original ASPC WNN UMAP coordinates. Condition-specific pseudotime distributions were calculated in a patient-first manner. Density estimates were calculated for each individual on a common pseudotime grid, after which densities were averaged within condition and displayed together with the standard error of the mean across patients. Mean pseudotime was likewise calculated first for each patient and subsequently summarized across patients.

## RNA pseudobulk differential-expression analysis

Differential gene expression between CXCL14+ and PPARG+ ASPCs was assessed using patient-level pseudobulk counts. Raw RNA counts were aggregated for each patient-population combination. All available patient-population samples were retained, including low-cell-number combinations, and genes with fewer than 10 total counts across the pseudobulk dataset were excluded. Differential expression was performed with DESeq2 using the design formula ~ patient + population, with PPARG+ ASPCs compared with CXCL14+ ASPCs. Positive log2 fold changes therefore indicate higher expression in PPARG+ ASPCs. Genes were considered differentially expressed at a Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P value below 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change greater than 0.5.

Gene Ontology Biological Process enrichment was performed separately for genes increased in CXCL14+ and PPARG+ ASPCs using enrichGO. The background universe consisted of all genes with a valid adjusted P value in the corresponding DESeq2 analysis. Multiple-testing correction was performed using the Benjamini-Hochberg method.

## Transcription-factor dynamics along adipogenic pseudotime

To identify transcription factors whose expression varied along the ASPC adipogenic continuum, ASPCs were divided into 12 equal-nucleus-number pseudotime bins. Raw RNA counts were aggregated for each patient-by-bin combination, and patient-bin combinations containing fewer than 20 nuclei were excluded. Genes with fewer than 10 total counts were removed. Transcription factors were defined using Gene Ontology molecular function term GO:0003700 (DNA-binding transcription factor activity).

Pseudotime-dependent expression was tested with a DESeq2 likelihood-ratio test. The full model was ~ patient + ns(Mean_pseudotime, df = 3), and the reduced model was ~ patient. Transcription factors with an adjusted P value below 0.05 were considered pseudotime-associated. Variance-stabilized expression values were used for visualization. For the heatmap, expression was averaged equally across patients within each pseudotime bin and row-wise z scores were calculated. Transcription factors were ordered according to the pseudotime bin of maximal expression. Spearman correlations with pseudotime were used descriptively and were not used to determine statistical significance.

## Differential chromatin accessibility

Differential chromatin accessibility between CXCL14+ and PPARG+ ASPCs was assessed by patient-level pseudobulk analysis of raw ATAC peak counts. Counts were aggregated for each patient-population combination and analyzed with DESeq2 using the design formula ~ patient + population. Peaks with fewer than 10 total counts were removed. Positive log2 fold changes indicate greater accessibility in PPARG+ ASPCs. Differentially accessible peaks (DAPs) were defined by an adjusted P value below 0.05 and an absolute log2 fold change greater than 0.5.

For directional accessibility profiles, CXCL14+-higher and PPARG+-higher DAPs were analyzed separately. Positional ATAC signal was calculated within 2 kb upstream and downstream of the DAP centers using Signac RegionMatrix functionality. To account for the marked difference in ATAC depth between the ASPC states, positional signal was normalized by the summed ATAC library size of the corresponding population and scaled to 10^6. The resulting depth-normalized matrices were used directly for aggregate accessibility profiles and heatmaps.

## Genomic annotation and transcription-factor motif enrichment

Directional DAPs were converted to genomic ranges and annotated with ChIPseeker using EnsDb.Hsapiens.v86. Promoter-proximal regions were defined as -3 kb to +3 kb relative to annotated transcription start sites, and genomic annotation was summarized separately for CXCL14+-higher and PPARG+-higher DAPs.

Motif analyses were performed with Signac using human CORE transcription-factor motifs and hg38 sequence information. Sequence statistics were calculated with RegionStats using BSgenome.Hsapiens.UCSC.hg38. Unbiased motif enrichment was performed independently for CXCL14+-higher and PPARG+-higher DAPs using FindMotifs with 40,000 background peaks and Benjamini-Hochberg multiple-testing correction. All available transcription-factor motifs were tested; the most significantly enriched motifs were displayed for visualization.

A focused regulatory-program analysis was subsequently performed for NR3C1, KLF15, STAT5B, CEBPD, CEBPB, CEBPA and PPARG::RXRA. Motif identities and fold enrichments were taken from the same unbiased motif analysis rather than from a separately fitted targeted model.

## Peak-gene linkage and motif-supported regulatory programs

Peak-gene associations were calculated with Signac LinkPeaks using the ATAC assay for chromatin accessibility and the RNA assay for gene expression. Candidate links were evaluated within 500 kb of the corresponding gene, with a minimum of 10 cells required. Links with P < 0.05 were retained for the focused regulatory-program analysis.

For each ASPC state, the denominator for the TF-program analysis was the number of unique genes linked to state-specific DAPs. For each transcription factor, the numerator was the number of those genes linked through DAPs containing the corresponding TF motif. The resulting percentage was used as a measure of the relative size of the motif-supported linked target program. In the focused bubble plot, point size represents the percentage of linked target genes and color represents the motif fold enrichment obtained from the unbiased enrichment analysis. This analysis identifies motif-supported regulatory associations and does not establish direct transcription-factor occupancy or direct TF-target binding.

The PPARG locus was visualized using Signac CoveragePlot to integrate RNA expression, ATAC accessibility, called peaks and peak-gene links. The PPARG-linked differential ATAC region at chr3:12,452,634-12,454,044 (GRCh38) was highlighted as an example of coordinated transcriptional and chromatin remodeling.

## Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Patients, rather than individual nuclei, were treated as biological replicates for group-level abundance, pseudotime and pseudobulk analyses. Differential RNA expression and differential chromatin accessibility were analyzed with DESeq2 and corrected for multiple testing using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. Unless otherwise specified, adjusted P values below 0.05 were considered significant. All analyses were performed in R using reproducible scripts, and intermediate Seurat/Signac objects were saved as analysis checkpoints to avoid recomputation and to preserve the exact dimensional reductions, cluster assignments and pseudotime values used for the manuscript figures.
